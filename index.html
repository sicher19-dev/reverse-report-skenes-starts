Mechanical / Soreness Triage  // read this before anything else

Every pitch was down, from the first one

The signature that matters: this isn’t a within-game fade. Four-seam velocity was 95.7–95.9 in every inning he threw it — flat, but flat about a full mph under where it lives. That flatness, present on the very first pitch of the game, points away from fatigue accumulating during the outing and toward something already present walking to the mound, or the pull was precautionary and stopped it before it became a within-game fade. Either way, the whole arsenal moved together, which argues for a body-wide cause rather than a single-pitch grip issue.

Specific Instances

Whole start (I1–I5) · extension short from pitch onePresent in every inning, not a fade
What
Extension was 6.60–6.73 ft across every pitch type tonight vs a 6.72–6.81 season band — 0.06 to 0.14 ft (roughly ¾″ to 1½″) short on every single pitch, and it was already short on his very first fastball of the game.
Likely Cause
Reduced extension from the opening pitch is the classic fingerprint of less drive off the back leg — not finishing the push down the mound, landing shorter than normal. This is the same pattern Derek’s mocap flagged in the 8/2 session (root cause #1: not enough load invested in the back leg).
Check Next Week
Back leg (drive-side hip/glute/hamstring) — strength and range of motion. If it’s sore or restricted rather than a timing issue, extension is where it would show up first, before velocity even drops.
3rd Inning · the home run pitchRun value +1.58, the single biggest swing of the night
What
First-pitch fastball to Esteury Ruiz: 95.5 mph (season 96.9), released from −2.49 ft (season −2.27, i.e. 2.6″ more toward the glove side than normal) and landed dead middle-middle (0.16, 2.93). Hit 105.1 mph for a home run.
Likely Cause
A release point shifted toward the glove side pulls misses toward the middle of the plate rather than to an edge — consistent with Derek’s root cause #2, the shoulder rotating/finishing early before the lower half is done, which drags the arm slot around the front of the body.
Check Next Week
Trunk rotation timing and front-side (lead-leg) blocking — whether the front knee/hip is stabilizing in time for the shoulders to fire on schedule, or opening early and pulling the release with it.
4th–5th Inning · sinker fell off late, on top of the whole-game deficit96.0 (I2–3) → 94.9 (I4) → 93.9 (I5)
What
The sinker specifically kept fading within the outing, layered on top of the game-long deficit: 96.0 in the 2nd/3rd, down to 94.9 in the 4th, down to 93.9 by the 5th — nearly 3 mph below its 96.7 season mark by the end. His final sinker of the night sailed to 4.26 ft high and 1.75 ft off the plate, hitting Esteury Ruiz.
Likely Cause
A pitch that relies on seam-shifted wake and precise pronation is the first to go when arm speed and finish erode further under fatigue stacked on an already-short outing — the wild high miss (not a location miss, a full-body miss) is more consistent with losing the release window entirely than a simple aim error.
Check Next Week
Forearm/wrist pronation strength and endurance under fatigue, plus posterior shoulder (rotator cuff/scap stability) for maintaining a consistent finish deep into an outing. This is the pitch and the window worth flagging hardest to the training staff.
Reading the tiers: Derek’s mocap work identified three Skenes velocity tiers — 100 / 97 / 94 — tied to back-hip muscle recruitment, not arm mechanics. Tonight’s 95.7–95.9 average sits almost exactly on the line between the 97 and 94 tiers — closer to the low tier than any tracked start this season. Combined with short extension from pitch one and a glove-side release shift, this reads as a lower-half access problem present before the start began, not one that developed during it. Recommend: full mobility/soreness screen on the back leg and hip before the next bullpen, prioritized over any mechanical cue work.

The Bottom Line

The line was fine. The engine wasn't.

One earned run and four strikeouts on 65 pitches is a start most nights would call a success. The mechanical picture underneath says something else was going on from the first pitch, and the training staff pulling him at a low pitch count with clean process results is consistent with that read. This is a body-first week, not a mound-work week.

Keep

The results, for what they’re worth — command was fine (1 BB), the splitter closed the day strong, and the defense/review system helped keep the damage to one run. None of that should distract from what the metrics say underneath.

Fix
